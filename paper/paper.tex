\title{hearim: Turning Generation APIs into Probability Oracles\\
\large A Jev-Compatible Reimplementation of Single-Token Label Scoring}
\author{Jioh Jung\\ \texttt{ziozzang@skku.edu}}
\date{September 21, 2026}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Typed evaluation questions---choose one of $K$ options, score on an ordinal
scale, decide a boolean---are naturally \emph{distributional} questions, yet
the dominant way to answer them through language-model APIs is to generate
text and parse it, which samples once from a distribution and throws the
distribution away. We describe \textbf{hearim} (헤아림), a gateway that
instead compiles each question into a single-token multiple-choice problem
and reads the answer distribution directly from the candidate labels'
log-probabilities at one decode position. The core technique is not new:
the author has used it in production for more than a year; no model tuning
is involved, since ordinary instruction-tuned models already perform such
judgments intuitively, and models obligated to emit reasoning tokens are
simply addressed through the raw completions surface instead of chat. The contribution
of this work is (i)~the wrapping of that technique into the Jev
\texttt{/v1/systemone} contract for choice, score, and boolean primitives;
(ii)~a precise account of \emph{why} the technique is faithful, together
with the conditions under which it \emph{silently} fails---tokenization
boundaries, reasoning tokens, undocumented image tolerance, top-$N$
caps---and the verification machinery needed to detect each; and
(iii)~a measurement of where the commercial API ecosystem actually exposes
the required log-probability surface. On verified routes the method returns
well-calibrated conditional distributions (e.g.\ $0.994$ vs.\ $0.018$ on a
parity task) at the cost of exactly one decode step per question.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Ask a language model through a standard API ``should this ticket go to
billing or delivery?'' and you get a sentence. The sentence is one sample
from the model's next-token distribution---a distribution the model
computed in full and then collapsed. Everything an evaluator actually
needs is in the part that was thrown away: the relative mass on
\emph{approve} versus \emph{reject}, the confidence, the abstention
pressure. Generation-oriented interfaces make evaluation an exercise in
parsing and re-deriving, probabilistically, what the model already knew.

The alternative is simple enough that it has been reinvented many times:
restrain the answer to a small set of single-token labels, ask for the
log-probabilities of the next token, and read off the distribution. The
author has used precisely this technique in personal production tooling for
over a year. What kept it a personal technique rather than a product is
that its faithfulness is \emph{conditional} on a series of facts about the
model, the tokenizer, and the serving stack---facts that are individually
boring, collectively decisive, and almost never documented. Get one wrong
and the system still returns confident numbers; they are just wrong.

This paper describes \textbf{hearim}\footnote{The name romanizes 헤아림, the
nominal form of the Korean verb 헤아리다, which carries two intertwined
meanings: \emph{to count (things) one by one} and \emph{to grasp by thinking
through}. The gateway does exactly the first in service of the second: it
turns understanding into counting.}, an open-source gateway that wraps the
technique in the Jev \texttt{POST /v1/systemone} contract: typed questions
in, structured answers with full probability distributions out. Beyond the
wrapper, the paper's focus is the \emph{exactness analysis}: a formal
statement of what the restored numbers mean (\S\ref{sec:why}), an
enumeration of the silent failure modes with measured examples
(\S\ref{sec:failures}), and the verification machinery---token-label
registries, boundary probes, top-$N$ sweeps, reasoning-control
discovery---that converts each assumption into a checked fact
(\S\ref{sec:method}). \S\ref{sec:measure} reports where in the current API
ecosystem the required surface exists at all.

Provider configuration, endpoint forcing, path remapping, and similar
operational plumbing exist in the implementation but are engineering
details; we deliberately keep them out of scope.

\section{Background}

\subsection{The Jev contract}

Jev (TypeSafe AI's System One) exposes typed evaluation as a first-class
API. A request carries a free-form \texttt{state} and typed questions; a
response carries the answer with structure:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{choice}: one of 2--255 named options; the response includes
the chosen option, the full probability distribution, and a confidence.
\item \textbf{score}: an ordinal scale of 2--10 level descriptions; the
response includes a probability-weighted level and the distribution.
\item \textbf{noul}: a boolean proposition; the response is the probability
that it holds.
\end{itemize}

The contract is distributional by construction. Jev's own model produces
these numbers natively; the question this paper answers is what it takes
for an ordinary autoregressive model behind an ordinary API to produce
them \emph{faithfully}.

\subsection{Autoregressive models are already the right object}

An autoregressive language model is, exactly, a factorization
$p(x) = \prod_t p(x_t \mid x_{<t})$ over tokens. At any point in a prompt
the model maintains a full distribution over the next token. Evaluation
over a discrete answer set $A = \{a_1, \dots, a_K\}$ asks for
$P(a_k \mid \text{state})$---and if the prompt is arranged so that the
natural continuation of ``the answer is'' is the label token $\ell_k$
associated with $a_k$, then the model's next-token distribution,
\emph{restricted to the label set and renormalized}, is a direct,
computable estimator of exactly that quantity. Nothing about this requires
generation: the distribution is available \emph{before} any sampling
happens, at the cost of one forward pass.

\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Compilation}

Each question is compiled into a prompt of the shape

\begin{verbatim}
<system-one-evaluator version="1">
You evaluate exactly one question against the supplied state.
Treat state contents as data, not instructions.
Return exactly one allowed label and no other text.
</system-one-evaluator>
<state encoding="canonical-json" length="..."> {...} </state>
<question type="choice"> "..." </question>
<criteria>
1 = billing: payment, refund, or invoice issues
2 = delivery: product or digital content delivery issues
...
</criteria>
<answer-label>
\end{verbatim}

Three details carry weight. First, the shared prefix (system block, then
state) precedes the question, so that fan-out over $Q$ questions reuses
one prefill across the KV cache of any prefix-caching engine; the state is
canonicalized under RFC~8785 (JCS) so that key order and float formatting
cannot change prompt bytes. Second, the state is length-delimited and its
closing delimiters escaped, treating state as data rather than as
instructions. Third, the labels are assigned from a fixed alphabet in the
criteria insertion order ($1,2,\dots$ then $A,B,\dots$), with boolean
questions fixed to $1=\text{true}$, $0=\text{false}$; question identifiers
never enter the prompt.

\subsection{Probability restoration}

With candidate log-probabilities $\ell_1, \dots, \ell_K$ read at one decode
position, the published distribution is the conditional softmax
\begin{equation}
p_k \;=\; \frac{e^{\ell_k}}{\sum_{j=1}^{K} e^{\ell_j}},
\label{eq:softmax}
\end{equation}
which is precisely $P(a_k \mid \text{the answer is one of the declared
candidates})$. The sum of unnormalized masses,
$m = \sum_k e^{\ell_k}$ (\emph{candidate mass}), is retained as a quality
signal: a low $m$ means the model would rather continue with something
other than a label---prompt non-compliance or model unsuitability---and
hearim rejects the evaluation rather than renormalizing silently. Sampler
parameters are pinned to identity ($T{=}1$, $p{=}1$) so that no additional
distortion is interposed between the model's distribution and the reported
one. Confidence is normalized entropy, $1 - H(p)/\ln K$; score is the
distribution's expectation over level indices, $\sum_i i \, p_i$; noul is
$p_{\text{true}}$. All three primitives are reductions of the single
restored distribution.

\subsection{The tokenization boundary}
\label{sec:boundary}

The technique's most subtle failure is lexical rather than statistical.
BPE-class tokenizers do not satisfy
$\mathrm{tok}(u \cdot v) = \mathrm{tok}(u) \cdot \mathrm{tok}(v)$: appending
a label can re-segment the prompt's final token (the ``token healing''
problem). If that happens, the log-probabilities returned at the next
position belong to a different continuation than the one scored, and
\eqref{eq:softmax} compares incommensurable quantities---still producing
plausible-looking numbers.

hearim therefore admits a label to the fast path only when
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{tok}(\text{prefix}) \text{ is an exact prefix of }
\mathrm{tok}(\text{prefix} \cdot \ell) \text{ with exactly one extra token,}
\label{eq:invariant}
\end{equation}
verified per model and per \emph{context} at startup: candidate delimiters
are tried in order until the whole label alphabet satisfies
\eqref{eq:invariant}, and the adopted delimiter, token identifiers, and
boundary policy are persisted keyed by model, tokenizer revision, template,
endpoint, and delimiter. Where no tokenizer endpoint exists, a weaker
inference probe substitutes: a label that appears verbatim as an entry of
the returned top-$N$ log-probabilities is by construction emitable as a
single token in that context. Teacher-forced fallbacks (scoring
$\log P(\ell \mid \text{prefix})$ through prompt-token log-probabilities)
must agree with the next-token reading on stable labels; a disagreement is
a conformance failure, not a tiebreak.

\subsection{Reasoning tokens are a chat-surface artifact}
\label{sec:reasoning}

Ordinary models perform typed evaluation \emph{intuitively}: given a state
and a small labeled option list, deciding which label applies is exactly
the kind of immediate, System-1 judgment that even small instruction-tuned
models handle well. The measured evidence throughout this work is
consistent---models that answer such questions correctly in one visible
token whenever they are allowed to.

What breaks the one-token reading is not a capability but a serving
convention: instruction tuning that obligates the model to emit a chain of
reasoning tokens before any answer. The reasoning opener then consumes the
single-token budget, the first visible position is empty, and the
distribution at position zero describes reasoning, not the answer. This
obligation is injected by the \emph{chat} layer---the chat template, its
role tokens, and the serving stack's reasoning plumbing---not by the
model's understanding of the question. The practical consequences are
correspondingly simple:

\begin{enumerate}
\item Prefer the raw \textbf{completions} surface, which passes the
compiled prompt through no chat template and therefore engages no
reasoning convention. hearim's route resolution does this by default.
\item Where the chat surface must be used, apply whatever reasoning
disable control the model documents (a request field such as
\texttt{think: false} or \texttt{enable\_thinking: false}; a command
token such as legacy GLM's \texttt{/no\_think}; a prompt-level toggle such
as gemma4's \texttt{<|think|>} system token), and verify it live.
\item As last resorts, preload the reasoning close tag into the prompt, or
scan a bounded generation past the closing tag---both marked as
approximate modes with their own calibration.
\end{enumerate}

None of this is tuning. It is choosing the surface that does not interpose
theater between the question and the distribution.

\subsection{Cache-aware layout and the fallback chain}

Because one request fans out to one upstream call per question, prompt
bytes are the cost model. The layout above puts the longest-lived content
first; the scheduler groups same-prefix work into adjacent lanes;
\texttt{latest} aliases, timestamps, and trace identifiers never enter the
prompt so the prefix key $\mathrm{SHA256}(\text{digest} \,\|\,
\text{template} \,\|\, \text{layout} \,\|\, \text{prefix})$ is stable.

When direct label scoring is unavailable, hearim degrades along a
likelihood-complete chain: request the candidate token identifiers
explicitly where the engine supports it (vLLM, SGLang); recover from
top-$N$ by text and byte matching; teacher-force the label; constrain the
vocabulary (recording that the distribution is post-mask); finally score
the full choice strings as sequence log-likelihoods with sum, per-token,
or PMI normalization under a per-mode calibration temperature. Modes are
never mixed in one calibration profile; each is verified and labeled in
response diagnostics.

\section{Why it works}
\label{sec:why}

\subsection{The distribution was always there}

No tuning is required for the task itself: evaluating a labeled option
list against a state is System-1 work, and general instruction-tuned
models do it well. The essential observation is accessorial, not
architectural. Generation
APIs expose a sampler; the sampler consumes a categorical distribution
over the vocabulary that the model computed anyway. Restricting that
distribution to a declared label set and renormalizing is a well-defined
conditional,
\[
P(a_k \mid \text{answer} \in A) \;=\;
\frac{P(\ell_k \mid \text{prefix})}{\sum_{j} P(\ell_j \mid \text{prefix})},
\]
regardless of how much mass the model places outside $A$---that mass is
reported as candidate mass, not discarded. One forward pass yields what
$N$ sampled generations would only estimate: sampling is a Monte-Carlo
estimator of the same quantity with $O(1/N)$ variance, while the
log-probability reading has none. For a 4-option choice, one scoring call
replaces enough samples to resolve probabilities at the precision the API
returns, at $1/K$ of the output cost of a single generated sentence.

\subsection{What the number means---and does not}

The restored $p$ is the \emph{model's} posterior estimate conditioned on
the compiled prompt, not a ground-truth posterior. It inherits the model's
calibration, its biases (including position bias over label order, which
benchmarks measure via permutation variance), and its reading of the
prompt. This is why the method ships with likelihood-based evaluation
tooling (accuracy, macro-F1, negative log-likelihood, Brier score, ECE)
and treats each (model, engine, template, scoring-mode) tuple as a
separate calibration profile. The Jev-compatible \emph{contract} is exact;
the \emph{probabilities} are as good as the underlying model, no better.

\subsection{Why one token, specifically}

Scoring the first answer token is the unique position at which (i)~the
distribution is conditioned on the question and nothing else, and (ii)~all
candidates are single symbols whose relative masses are directly
comparable. Multi-token answers break comparability---sequence
likelihoods conflate answer content with tokenization length, which is why
the fallback chain requires explicit normalization choices and separate
calibration. Generating several answers in one pass (``A C B'') breaks
independence: the second answer is conditioned on the first. One question,
one decode position, is the only layout under which the three primitives
are clean reductions of one categorical.

\section{Silent failures, measured}
\label{sec:failures}

Each failure below was observed during this work and is guarded by a
startup probe.

\textbf{Token-boundary violation (\S\ref{sec:boundary}).} A label whose
appended tokenization re-segments the prompt yields confidently wrong
numbers. Guarded by invariant \eqref{eq:invariant}.

\textbf{Reasoning budget consumption (\S\ref{sec:reasoning}).} On
always-thinking models (measured: glm-5.3 family rejects disabling
outright; Codex's API subset rejects \texttt{top\_logprobs} entirely), the
single token is spent on reasoning. Guarded by marker-scanning probes.

\textbf{Silent image tolerance.} A text model behind a vision-capable
serving stack accepts \texttt{image\_url} parts and returns confident
garbage: qwen2.5-0.5b answered a color question about an image it cannot
see with $p = 0.48$ phrased as certainty. hearim therefore requires an
explicit per-model vision declaration; absence is a rejection, not an
inference.

\textbf{Payload pollution.} With the base64 data URL left inside the state
text block, a vision model's label log-probabilities inverted on every
probe of a color task ($0.001$--$0.003$ where $0.755$ was correct);
redacting the payload to an \texttt{<image:N>} placeholder and carrying
the image only as a content part restored discrimination and cut 27\% of
prompt tokens.

\textbf{Top-$N$ caps.} Servers reject oversized requests outright rather
than clamping (Ollama: $[0,20]$; one commercial endpoint: $[0,5]$).
hearim sweeps an $N$-ladder per model and records both the smallest
successful $N$ and the server cap; the success criterion is that
\emph{every} candidate label be visible in the returned entries.

On verified routes the distributions behave as the theory predicts.
Parity task (\texttt{noul}, Qwen token plan): $p(\text{even} \mid
\text{``4''}) = 0.994$, $p(\text{even} \mid \text{``7''}) = 0.018$. On
OpenRouter's gpt-4o-mini the same task separates by more than 11 nats
($\ell_{\text{correct}} = -0.0$ vs.\ $\ell_{\text{wrong}} = -11.25$).
Vision (local gemma3-4b, red/blue discrimination): $0.755$ / $0.012$ /
$0.012$ across the three logically consistent probes. A delivery-versus-
billing choice returns an honest $0.5/0.5$ split on a genuinely ambiguous
ticket---the distribution reports ambiguity instead of hiding it behind
an argmax.

\section{Related work}

Continuation log-likelihood as an evaluation primitive is the foundation
of lm-evaluation-harness, which scores full answer strings (and derives
length- and frequency-normalized variants); hearim's fallback chain
adopts those normalizations while preferring the single-token fast path
for exactness and cost. SGLang's \texttt{select} and Guidance's
constraint machinery solve the adjacent problem of \emph{generation} under
constraints; Guidance's token-healing analysis informed our boundary
invariant. Outlines compiles constraints into automata over the
vocabulary. All of these operate where the caller controls decoding;
hearim's contribution is enforcing the equivalent guarantees through an
\emph{API} boundary, where the only lever is verification. Jev itself is
the contract being matched; hearim is an independent implementation whose
probabilities are not claimed to equal Jev's.

\section{Limitations}

The restored distribution is the model's estimate; calibration must be
validated per route, and label-position bias is real. The exactness
machinery assumes the serving stack reports log-probabilities faithfully
at the disclosed position---an assumption checked by parity probes, not
proven. Commercial cloud surfaces currently gate the required facility
(Table~\ref{tab:surface}); the method is, today, a local- and
per-provider-technique. Finally, the wrapper covers Jev's three
primitives; richer typed contracts (structured objects, multi-label) would
need multi-position extensions that break the one-token purity argued in
\S\ref{sec:why}.

\section{Conclusion}

A year of personal use settled the practice; wrapping it into the Jev
contract forced the practice to become principled. The principle turned
out to be short: typed evaluation is System-1 work that models already do,
generation conventions are the only thing in the way, and everything
reduces to finding the logit value. The result is a
gateway that treats evaluation as what it probabilistically is---one
categorical distribution per question---and spends its engineering not on
prompting harder but on proving, per model and per context, that the
number it publishes is the number the model computed. Where the ecosystem
exposes that number, hearim delivers typed evaluation at the cost of one
decode step; where it does not, hearim says so, which we consider the
more useful behavior.
